# ADR-003 — The microphone cuts the capture into utterances

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-05, commit `ef25ade`)

## Context
Silence detection lived in STT, which received every chunk. That sent audio nobody needed, put a capture concern in a transcription service and forced STT to know the microphone's volume scale.

## Decision
Silence detection moves here (`UtteranceSegmenter`). The stream carries one `utterance` event per utterance with its whole audio, at `MICROPHONE_RESAMPLE_TO_HZ` when set. The cutting is always on; each treatment (DC offset removal, volume smoothing, noise-floor tracking, resampling) is an environment variable and a field of `InputTreatment`, validated at start. Defaults: threshold 150, limit 2.0 s, start factor 2.0, smoothing on (0.1), noise-floor tracking on, DC offset off, no resample.

## Consequences
- Positive: STT only transcribes; less traffic; the thresholds are tuned where the audio is captured.
- Negative: the utterance in progress is lost if the device fails; the defaults are not tuned on real rooms yet (hardware retest pending); the resample is linear interpolation, not a filtered converter.

> **Update 2026-11-09: a pre-roll in front of each utterance (`MICROPHONE_PREROLL_SECONDS`, default 0.2 s).** The cutter started an utterance only at the chunk where the (smoothed) volume reached the start volume, so the opening of the first word was cut off: the volume needs a moment to reach the start volume (more with smoothing), so the first sounds of the first word were lost: on 2026-11-09 the wake-phrase gate of stt heard `What time is it?` for `Oblivion 306, what time is it?` and accepted only 2 of 8 phrases live; re-cutting one real recording offline showed the cutter starting 0.01 to 0.12 s after the voiced onset and the same gate accepting 5 of 10, against 9 or 10 of 10 when the clip starts about 0.1 s before the onset (see stt ADR-005). The segmenter now keeps the latest quiet chunks in a small rolling buffer (whole chunks, at least `preroll_seconds`, nothing buffered while an utterance is in progress) and puts them in front of the utterance when it starts, resampled with it. It changes no event and no contract: an `utterance` event just starts a little earlier. Consequences: utterances are about 0.26 s longer at the defaults; the thresholds, the smoothing and the end of an utterance are unchanged; set `MICROPHONE_PREROLL_SECONDS=0` for the old behaviour. Evidence so far is the offline diagnosis and fake-audio tests (142 pass, 12 new, 10 of 10 one-line mutations caught); the new cutter was not yet run with a real microphone.

> **Update 2026-11-09 (verification of the pre-roll):** Offline check of the pre-roll on a second recording of the maintainer (10 phrases, 3 sentences, 110 s, no services running): each phrase was fed alone to a fresh copy of the real changed `UtteranceSegmenter` with the live default treatment, and what came out was scored with stt's gate (`base`, prompt `Oblivion 306.`, beam 1) and Brain's matcher. Phrases accepted of 10: pre-roll 0 (the old behaviour) 8, 0.1 s 10, 0.2 s (the new default) 9, 0.3 s 8, 0.5 s 9. That is neutral to slightly positive and **within the noise of 10 phrases**: this recording does not prove a gain, because the old cutter already did well on it (it started 0.0 to 0.1 s after the voiced onset, where the first recording's controlled sweep showed the cliff between starting 0.1 s inside the phrase, 3 to 5 of 10, and at or before the onset, 8 to 10 of 10). The pre-roll protects against the unlucky starts (a soft first sound, more smoothing delay) and was not seen to hurt. Also seen: with the default smoothed volume, phrases said about 5 s apart are **merged** into one utterance (the smoothed volume needs about 1.8 s to fall under the end threshold, plus the 2 s silence limit, so the end of an utterance is detected 3.8 s after the speech; one cut was 46 s long); with smoothing off every phrase was its own utterance (4.5 s each). Not yet run: the live round trip through Brain with the new microphone.

> **Update 2026-11-09 (live check of the pre-roll):** Third live test, the same day, with both fixes (microphone pre-roll 0.2 s, gate `base` with prompt `Oblivion 306.`, read from the new `.env.example` files; microphone, stt, tts, speaker and Brain from copies of the working trees, no ai-agent, no stepper), phrases said 8 to 10 s apart: Brain received 11 utterances. **Every phrase that reached the gate was accepted: 4 of 4**, each followed by exactly one `/process/batch` (3.8 to 4 s with `small.en`, 31 to 33 characters back, then Brain's fixed apology because ai-agent was not running), and the one ordinary sentence that reached the gate (`The weather is nice today and I like coffee.`) was ignored correctly. The other **6 utterances never reached the gate**: Brain's echo guard dropped them (`utterance heard while the robot was speaking dropped`, 5.6 to 6.5 s each), because every accepted phrase makes the robot speak for a long time (its progress messages and the apology), and the maintainer said the next phrase or sentence during it (4 phrases and 2 ordinary sentences). So the live results with the same gate have been: first test 1 of 3, second 2 of 8, third 4 of 4 reaching the gate. The sample is small (4 phrases), the speaker and room are one, and the three runs differ in more than the two fixes (pace, what was said while the robot spoke), but this is the first run with no phrase lost to a misheard name. Not measured: a run with a real ai-agent (the robot's reply would be different and shorter or longer), a Raspberry Pi, other voices or noise. The pre-roll was part of this run.

> **Update 2026-11-10 (first run on a real microphone since the cutting moved here):** with the defaults on a headset microphone (speech RMS 1,000 to 4,000, room noise RMS 4 to 80), three sentences about 8 s apart became three utterances of 7.42, 5.38 and 4.93 s through `/stream`, each ending on its own, 0 overflows. About 3.4 s of every utterance is trailing silence (the 2 s silence limit plus the 1.4 s the smoothed volume needs to fall under the end threshold), so a reply cannot start earlier than that after the sentence ends. Clipped starts were not assessed (no audio was kept). Details: T-04.1.

> **Update 2026-11-10 (T-04.2, offline re-cut of a real recording; no default changed):** a 159 s recording from the real headset microphone was re-cut with the real segmenter under 24 treatments (the recording was deleted afterwards). At the defaults no sentence start was clipped (the pre-roll starts the cut at least 0.13 s before the voiced onset) and the cost is **3.4 s of trailing silence** per utterance; phrases merge only when the quiet between them is under about 3.7 s (the merge of phrases "about 5 s apart" of 2026-11-09 was not reproduced: this recording's closest gap was 4.3 s). Smoothing factor 0.3 with a 1.0 s silence limit gave 1.4 s of trailing silence with no merge, no split and no clipped start; a 1.0 s limit without smoothing, or 0.7 s with it, split a sentence with an inner pause. The risk of the faster smoothing (a click or cough starting an utterance) is untested. **Proposed, not decided:** `MICROPHONE_VOLUME_SMOOTHING_FACTOR=0.3` and `MICROPHONE_SILENCE_LIMIT_SECONDS=1.0` in the maintainer's local file first, a change of the code defaults only after a live check and a noise check.

> **Update 2026-11-10 (T-04.2 closed: the defaults are confirmed):** the faster setting proposed above (smoothing factor 0.3, silence limit 1.0 s) was run live and the same loud noises (taps, coughs, a clap, a knock; 45 s, no speech, recorded and deleted) were re-cut under many treatments: the defaults cut 4 false utterances (23.7 s of audio), the proposal 8 (17.7 s), smoothing off 8 to 10, and a start volume of 300 or 600 only 3 or 2; no treatment avoids false triggers from loud noises. The 2 s the proposal saves cost twice the false utterances, and the effect of a higher start volume on soft speech was not measured, so **no default changes**. The numbers and the option for a local setting are in T-04.2. Not measured: soft or distant speech, other rooms or voices, the wake-phrase scoring.
