## Goal

Make the defaults of `InputTreatment` right for the room and microphone of the maintainer, or confirm them.

## What it must do

- From the *Results* of T-04.1, list the symptom of each failure and the setting that addresses it:
  - clipped starts: raise `MICROPHONE_PREROLL_SECONDS` (default 0.2 since 2026-11-09, ADR-003), or lower `MICROPHONE_SILENCE_THRESHOLD` or `MICROPHONE_SPEECH_START_FACTOR`;
  - an utterance that never ends in a noisy room: raise the threshold, or check `MICROPHONE_NOISE_FLOOR_TRACKING` (a floor that stays too high in a very quiet room also hurts: turn it off);
  - a click starts an utterance: lower `MICROPHONE_VOLUME_SMOOTHING_FACTOR` (more smoothing) or raise `MICROPHONE_SPEECH_START_FACTOR`;
  - answers feel slow: lower `MICROPHONE_SILENCE_LIMIT_SECONDS`;
  - STT accuracy: `MICROPHONE_RESAMPLE_TO_HZ=16000`.
- Prefer to change the **machine's local settings** (`deployment/config/local/microphone.toml`) over the code default; change the code default only if the maintainer says the new value is right for every machine (a decision, recorded in a dated update of ADR-003).
- If a default changes: `domain/value_objects/input_treatment.py`, `.env.example`, `README.md`, `docs/configuration/environment-variables.md`, `docs/architecture/architecture.md` (sections 7 and 8), `docs/setup/08_domain-modeling.md`, `docs/setup/14_risks-and-debt.md`, `tests/domain/test_input_treatment.py` (and any test that relies on the old value), `deployment/docs/DEPLOYMENT.md` and `deployment/config/services/microphone.toml` (regenerate).

## What it must not do

- Change a default without a result from T-04.1 that supports it.
- Test "in the room" with unit tests; the unit tests only check the values and the rules.

## Acceptance

The defaults are confirmed or changed with a recorded reason; the documents agree; the baseline of the tasks README section 3 passes; the maintainer confirms on the real microphone that the symptom is gone (a second manual run, recorded in *Results*).

## Update 2026-11-09 (plan revision 2): what is already known

- **Finding F10 (ADR-003, second update):** with the default smoothed volume (`MICROPHONE_VOLUME_SMOOTHING=1`, factor 0.1) phrases said about 5 s apart merge into one utterance, because the smoothed volume needs about 1.8 s to fall under the end threshold, plus `MICROPHONE_SILENCE_LIMIT_SECONDS` (2 s): the end is seen 3.8 s after the speech; with smoothing off each phrase was its own utterance (4.5 s each). Candidates to measure: smoothing off, a smaller `MICROPHONE_SILENCE_LIMIT_SECONDS`, a larger `MICROPHONE_VOLUME_SMOOTHING_FACTOR` (less smoothing); the pre-roll stays at 0.2 s (its sweep on 10 phrases was within the noise, 0.1 s was best, 8 to 10 of 10 accepted).
- **Method (the one that found the pre-roll problem):** record continuous audio with the maintainer present, then re-cut it **offline** with the real `UtteranceSegmenter` and the treatment under test, and score each cut (utterance count against phrases said, merged or split phrases, how many reach stt's wake gate: `base`, prompt `Oblivion 306.`, beam 1, with Brain's matcher). Score offline before any live run. The recording is the maintainer's voice: keep it in the scratchpad only, delete it when finished, never in the repository or a document.
- Q02-8: until this task has numbers, only the maintainer's `config/local/microphone.toml` changes; a code default changes only after the numbers and a dated update of ADR-003.

## Input from T-04.1 (2026-11-10, real headset microphone)

Measured with the defaults (threshold 150, start factor 2, silence limit 2.0 s, smoothing on 0.1, noise floor on, pre-roll 0.2 s): speech RMS 1,000 to 4,000 (peaks to 27,000), room noise RMS 4 to 80, three sentences about 8 s apart became three utterances of 7.42, 5.38 and 4.93 s: **about 3.4 s of every utterance is trailing silence** (2 s limit plus 1.4 s for the smoothed volume to fall), so the answer to a sentence can start no earlier than 3.4 s after it ends. Candidates: a smaller `MICROPHONE_SILENCE_LIMIT_SECONDS` and/or smoothing off (the merge of phrases about 5 s apart is the other side of the same trade). Clipped starts were **not** assessed (no audio kept): the offline method above is how.

## Result (2026-11-10, first part)

**What was done (2026-11-10, the maintainer at the PC, a "go" before the recording):** one continuous recording of 159.4 s from the real headset microphone (device 1, 16 kHz mono, 0 overflows, one beep as the start cue) into a temporary file of the scratchpad, **re-cut offline with the real `UtteranceSegmenter`** under 24 treatments (smoothing on or off, silence limit 2.0, 1.5, 1.0 and 0.7 s, smoothing factor 0.1 and 0.3, pre-roll 0.2 and 0.3 s; `scratchpad/recut.py`) and scored against the voiced segments of the recording itself. **The recording was deleted when the analysis was done** (checked); no transcription, no network and no gate model were used, so the wake-phrase scoring of the earlier method was **not** repeated here; what is printed are numbers only.

**The recording:** noise floor (10th percentile chunk RMS) 3, speech 90th percentile RMS 5,001; the maintainer said about 12 sentences and the analysis found **10 voiced segments** (two very short ones may have fused with a neighbour or been below 0.3 s), lengths 0.3 to 3.1 s, preceded by quiet of 6.7, 5.9, 5.8, 7.1, 5.6, 31.4, 4.3, 4.6 and 23.4 s (so the closest pair is 4.3 s apart, not the 3 s or less that merges at the default).

| smoothing | limit | factor | pre-roll | utterances (of 10 voiced) | merged | split | missed | clipped starts | earliest start before the onset | trailing silence mean / max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| on (**default**) | 2.0 s | 0.1 | 0.2 s | 9 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0.13 s | **3.40 / 3.65 s** |
| on | 2.0 | 0.3 | 0.2 | 9 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0.13 | 2.40 / 2.50 |
| on | 1.5 | 0.3 | 0.2 | 9 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0.13 | 1.89 / 1.98 |
| on | **1.0** | **0.3** | 0.2 | 9 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0.13 | **1.38 / 1.47** |
| on | 0.7 | 0.3 | 0.2 | 10 | 0 | **1** | 1 | 0 | 0.13 | 1.08 / 1.15 |
| off | 2.0 | - | 0.2 | 9 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0.19 | 2.08 / 2.18 |
| off | 1.5 | - | 0.2 | 9 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0.19 | 1.57 / 1.66 |
| off | 1.0 | - | 0.2 | 10 | 0 | **1** | 1 | 0 | 0.19 | 1.06 / 1.15 |

(The full 24 rows are the same for pre-roll 0.3 s, which only moves the earliest start to 0.19 s, or 0.26 s with smoothing off.)

**What it says.** (1) **No sentence start was clipped by any treatment**, the default included: the cutter always started at least 0.13 s before the voiced onset, so the pre-roll does its job and 0.2 s is enough on this recording. (2) **The default's cost is the trailing silence: 3.4 s on average**, the 2 s limit plus the 1.4 s the smoothed volume needs to fall under the end threshold; with smoothing off it is 2.1 s. (3) The phrases are merged only when the quiet between them is shorter than that trailing time: at the default **under about 3.7 s**, with smoothing factor 0.3 and a 1.0 s limit **under about 1.5 s**; this recording had no gap under 4.3 s, so the **merge of phrases "about 5 s apart" seen on 2026-11-09 was not reproduced** (that earlier recording had longer smoothing tails). (4) A limit of 1.0 s **without** smoothing, or 0.7 s with it, **splits** a sentence that has a pause of 1.0 to 1.5 s inside it (1 split here), which is worse for the speech-to-text engine than a late cut. (5) One 0.3 s sound was never cut by any treatment (a cough or a clipped word; not identified). (6) **Best on this recording: smoothing on, factor 0.3, silence limit 1.0 s: trailing silence 1.4 s instead of 3.4 s (2 s sooner), no merge, no split, no clipped start.** What it does **not** prove: factor 0.3 reacts three times faster to a click, a cough or a door (smoothing exists so one click is not speech), and this recording had no such noise, one voice, one room, one session of 10 segments. A false start by noise is **untested**.

**Decision (Q02-8): no code default was changed.** Until the maintainer decides, the defaults stay and only the maintainer's `deployment/config/local/microphone.toml` may change (`MICROPHONE_VOLUME_SMOOTHING_FACTOR = 0.3` and `MICROPHONE_SILENCE_LIMIT_SECONDS = 1.0`; I did not touch that file). The second manual run of the acceptance (a live check with those two settings, then a noise check) has **not** been run, so this task stays `in-progress`.

## Result (2026-11-10, second part: live check and noise)

**Live check with the proposed settings (smoothing factor 0.3, silence limit 1.0 s), the real service, the maintainer at the PC, a "go" before each opening, nothing saved to disk:**
- **Speech (60 s):** `stream_started`, three `utterance` events at 11.0, 23.1 and 35.5 s of 4.8, 3.7 and 4.1 s (peaks 19,886 to 22,299, RMS about 1,950), `completed` on `/stop`, 941 chunks, 0 overflows. The maintainer said his **timing went off** (the plan had three sentences and three noises, but there is no clock to follow), so this run shows only that speech is still cut into utterances with the faster settings; the noise part of it is **inconclusive**.
- **Noise only, same settings (40 s, no speech, about 6 loud noises: taps, a cough, a clap, a knock):** the service cut **5 utterances** of 2.6 to 4.7 s (peaks up to 32,768, full scale): **false triggers**, almost one per noise.

**Noise recording, re-cut offline (45 s of noises only, no speech, taps from soft to loud, coughs, a clap, a knock; deleted afterwards; `scratchpad/recut_noise.py`):** 10 noise events above RMS 150 (their loudest chunks RMS 313, 334, 2,068, 2,908, 13,269, 11,890, 5,507, 910, 653, 4,016) and 68 chunks over the start volume of 300. False utterances (and seconds of audio they would send to speech-to-text) by treatment:

| treatment | false utterances | seconds sent |
|---|---|---|
| **defaults** (smoothing on 0.1, limit 2.0, threshold 150, start factor 2) | **4** | 23.7 |
| smoothing 0.1, limit 1.0 | 5 | 19.2 |
| smoothing 0.3, limit 2.0 | 4 | 23.6 |
| **smoothing 0.3, limit 1.0 (the proposal)** | **8** | 17.7 |
| smoothing off, limit 2.0 | 8 | 26.9 |
| smoothing off, limit 1.0 | 10 | 18.3 |
| defaults but threshold 300 | 3 | 14.6 |
| defaults but threshold 600 | 2 | 10.6 |
| defaults but start factor 3 | 3 | 16.1 |
| smoothing 0.1, limit 1.0, threshold 600, start factor 3 | 2 | 6.5 |

**What it says.** (1) **No treatment avoids false triggers from loud noises** (a clap or a knock reaches RMS over 10,000, far over any start volume); the smoothing the defaults use only keeps the shortest and softest ones out. (2) **The proposal doubles them**: 8 false utterances against 4 at the defaults on the same noises, because a faster smoothing and a shorter silence limit both turn one long noise into more cuts (each is shorter, 17.7 s sent against 23.7 s). (3) **The trade of the proposal is therefore real**: 2 s less trailing silence (1.4 s against 3.4 s, T-04.1) for twice the false utterances on loud noise; a false utterance costs one speech-to-text call and is dropped by the wake-phrase gate in Brain, it does not reach the reply. (4) **Raising the start volume** (threshold 300 or 600) cuts the false triggers by a half or more but the effect on **soft speech** was not measured (the speech recording was already deleted; normal speech here is RMS 1,000 to 5,000 against 600, a soft or distant voice may not be).

**Decision (recorded reason): the defaults are confirmed; no code default was changed.** The 2 s that the proposal saves are paid with twice the false utterances, and the only treatments that cut the false utterances (a higher start volume) have an effect on soft speech that was not measured. The maintainer can overrule with settings in his local file; the trade is the table above (suggested only if the reply latency matters more than extra false utterances: `MICROPHONE_VOLUME_SMOOTHING_FACTOR = 0.3` and `MICROPHONE_SILENCE_LIMIT_SECONDS = 1.0`; or, to keep the false triggers at the default level, a limit of 1.5 s alone is **not** measured). Clipped sentence starts were not seen at any setting (T-04.2 first part). **What was not measured:** soft or distant speech against a higher threshold, other rooms, other voices, the wake-phrase scoring (`base` gate), so the numbers are for one room and one headset.
